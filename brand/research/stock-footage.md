# Stock footage for the long-form real opening (research, 2026-11-02)

Backs style-guide § 11.6.1 and brand.md § Long-form, "Real opening shot". Pages read by the brand-director on 2026-11-02. Licence terms change, so read the licence page again on the day you download a clip and write that date into the record (style-guide § 11.6.1, Licence record). Cited as SF§n.

## 1. Pexels License (read 2026-11-02)

Sources: `https://www.pexels.com/license/` (no date on the page) and `https://www.pexels.com/terms-of-service/` ("Last updated: October 20th, 2026"), § 5 and § 6.

- **Grant:** "irrevocable, worldwide, perpetual … non-exclusive and royalty-free". Covers commercial and non-commercial use (ToS § 5). Editing is allowed.
- **Attribution:** not required ("You do not need to credit Pexels or the contributor", ToS § 6).
- **Banned:** selling or distributing a clip **on a Standalone basis**, i.e. unaltered, as a file. Combining it into a "new" creative work is not standalone. Also banned: use in an immoral or illegal way, "especially Content which features a recognisable person"; commercial use, "in relation to goods and services", of content that shows trademarks, logos or brands; implying endorsement; using content as a trademark or business name; redistributing on other stock sites.
- **No warranty:** "We do not warrant that any consents or licenses have been obtained." Model and property releases are the contributor's duty (§ 7A), but responsibility "rests solely and exclusively with you".
- **Content ID:** not mentioned.
- ToS § 5 says "Non-CC0 downloads", so some Pexels items may be CC0. Record what the clip's own page says.

## 2. Pixabay Content License (read 2026-11-02)

Sources: `https://pixabay.com/service/license-summary/` and `https://pixabay.com/service/terms/` § 5 (no date on either page).

- **Grant:** use "for commercial or non-commercial purposes"; modification allowed; attribution not required.
- **Banned:** standalone sale or distribution ("where no creative effort has been applied to the Content and it remains in substantially the same form"); immoral or illegal use, "especially Content which features a recognisable person"; commercial use "in relation to goods and services" of content that shows trademarks, logos or brands; misleading or deceptive use; use as a trademark or trade name.
- **Third-party rights:** "you must consider whether you require the consent of a third party or a license". Pixabay disclaims this.
- **Content ID:** not mentioned in the terms. Third-party reviews say some Pixabay **music** carries a "Content ID registered" mark. That is music, not footage, and we never use stock audio anyway.

## 3. Content ID and non-exclusive stock

Source: YouTube Help, "Content eligible for Content ID", `https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/2605065` (read 2026-11-02).

- "You must have exclusive rights to the material in the reference file for the territories where you claim ownership."
- Content **not** eligible as a reference includes "Content licensed non-exclusively from a third party", "Content released under Creative Commons or similar free of charge/open licenses" and "Public domain footage".
- **[analysis]** So a Pexels or Pixabay clip should never be a valid Content ID reference. Contributors still upload the same clip to paid sites, or register it through aggregators, so claims happen anyway. Third-party blogs report this. They are not authoritative, and they are listed only as the reason for the pre-checks in § 11.6.1. Our protection is (a) the pre-checks and (b) a dated licence record, which wins a dispute.
- **YouTube Studio "Checks"** (upload flow, since March 2021, per YouTube Help `answer/57407` as reported by 9to5google and others): it screens for copyright before publishing. It does not cover claims from reference files added later.

## 4. Matching a drone lens to the house lens **[analysis]**

- Consumer drones shoot wide, about 24 mm equivalent, roughly 70 to 84° horizontal. The house lens is 26° horizontal (style-guide § 8, § 11.6). At the same building size, the two lenses give very different perspective, so a straight cross-fade would visibly warp.
- **Fix:** the 3D camera starts on the clip's solved lens. After the real image has gone, a **dolly-zoom** narrows the field of view to 26° while the camera pulls back, so the building keeps its size on screen and the perspective flattens into a section drawing. Telephoto drone clips (70 mm and up, about 30° or less) need little or no dolly-zoom. They are preferred, but they are rare on free sites.
- **Angle:** the house angle is about 28° of yaw off the cut face and about 16° down. The 3D building can be rotated to match any clip's yaw. Pitch can't be changed, so clips are limited to about 8 to 24° down (16 ± 8°). That way the small glide to the house angle during the reveal stays small.

## 5. Licences we don't accept, and why **[analysis]**

- **CC BY-NC:** non-commercial only. The channel is commercial (style-guide § 9, Licensing).
- **CC BY-ND:** we grade the clip, mask it and transition out of it. That is adapted material.
- **CC BY-SA:** share-alike could reach the whole video.
- **"Editorial use only"** clips, "personal use" licences, and anything taken from YouTube or another creator's post.
- **Not yet checked, so not allowed until a licence page is read and added here:** Mixkit, Coverr, Videvo (mixed licences per clip), and Wikimedia Commons (licence per file).
