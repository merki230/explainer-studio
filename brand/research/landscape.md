## 2. Patterns the successful ones share

| Pattern | Evidence | What it means for us |
|---|---|---|
| **Proposition in 3 s, hook within 6 s** | TikTok Ads guidance: "Introduce your content proposition in the first 3 seconds" and "Prioritize your hook in the first 6 seconds" ([TikTok for Business](https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/creative-best-practices?lang=en), last updated June 2025). Written for ads, but it is TikTok's only official timing guidance found. | The first line plus the first image must state the surprise. The current script's "Your phone is not listening to you. Probably not." is a good fit. |
| **The visual is the hook** | Zack D.: "the 'hook' is always visual" (weak source, above) | The first frame needs a striking 3D image, not a title card. |
| **Swipe-away is the first gate** | YouTube Studio's "viewed vs swiped away" metric (described by third-party guides, e.g. [ContentStudio](https://contentstudio.io/blog/youtube-shorts-analytics-guide); official help page not loaded) | Measure it per video. It is the hook scorecard. |
| **Replays count as views** | Since 2025-03-31, YouTube counts "the number of times a video starts to play or is replayed"; monetisation still uses "engaged views" ([Social Samosa, 2025-03-27](https://www.socialsamosa.com/news-2/youtube-shorts-now-count-every-play-replay-view-8895256)) | Loop endings (the last line flows into the first) raise view counts, but not RPM. |
| **Short still wins, even though longer is allowed** | YouTube Shorts allow up to 3 min ([Social Media Today, 2025-01-09](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/youtube-expands-3-minute-shorts-to-all-users/736967/)). Instagram Reels up to 3 min ([Business Today, 2025-01-21](https://www.businesstoday.in/technology/news/story/instagram-head-announces-big-changes-3-minute-reels-and-new-look-for-profile-grid-461527-2025-01-21)) | 60 s is a choice, not a limit. Keep 45–60 s for the main series. **[analysis]** |
| **Burned-in captions** | US 2019 survey of 5,616 adults: 69% watch video with sound off in public places, 25% in private ([3Play Media summary](https://www.3playmedia.com/blog/verizon-media-and-publicis-media-find-viewers-want-captions/) of Verizon Media/Publicis; the 69% figure was confirmed via other secondary coverage, not the original report). TikTok suggests "5-10 words per second" for on-screen text. | Keep word-synced captions. 160 words in 60 s is about 2.7 words/s, well under the limit. |
| **Volume and consistency** | Zack D.: about 33 posts a week on YT (CreatorDB). Kurzgesagt: about 2 a week. Branch: 0.2 a week. | Recognition comes from either volume (Zack) or a signature look plus trust (Kurzgesagt). We cannot match Zack's volume, so we need the look-plus-trust route. **[analysis]** |
| **Scene length / pacing** | No reliable source found. | **[unverified]** Fast cuts (a new visual every 2–4 s) are common practice. Test it against our retention data. |

## 3. Recurring worlds and mascots

| Channel | Device | Effect on recognition |
|---|---|---|
| Kurzgesagt | Bird mascots (incl. Duck) "in almost every video". Running gag: they die in escalating ways (Saratoga Falcon) | Strong. The birds carry the brand onto merch, and the gag rewards regular viewers. |
| King Science | The same crowned avatar "in every video" (Cartoon Brew) | Strong on TikTok. The character *is* the channel. |
| Life Noggin | Blocko mascot (Lifespan.io via snippet) | Moderate. Not verified in detail. |
| Nutshell Animations | Strict black-and-white palette (Cartoon Brew) | Shows that a palette alone can be the "world". |
| Zack D. Films | No mascot, just a consistent 3D render style and voice | Recognition through style and volume, which copycats now dilute (Fiverr and Upwork gigs). |

**What works [analysis]**
- The mascot is a **witness, not a host**. Kurzgesagt's birds react to the science; they don't explain it. They cost no screen time.
- **A fixed set across episodes** (same street, same apartment) lets returning viewers orient instantly and makes the new element pop.
- **Running gags** reward loyalty without blocking new viewers.
- **Palette discipline** gives recognition at thumbnail size.

**What feels gimmicky [analysis]**
- A mascot that talks to camera before the payoff wastes the 3-second window.
- Catchphrase intros or branded stings at the start hurt swipe rate. Put the brand moment at the end or loop point instead.
- Mascots that are cute but don't fit the topic (e.g. a joke character in a privacy video) undercut trust.
- A world so detailed that every episode must rebuild it (cost trap; see section 5).

## 4. Gaps

| Gap | Who covers it now | Opening for us |
|---|---|---|
| **Everyday *systems and services*** (ad targeting, loyalty cards, surge pricing, receipts, elevators, traffic lights, recycling sorting, delivery routing) | Branch, Jared Owen, Animagraffs and Lesics do *machines and objects*, long form. Zack D. leans body, history and shock. | 3D explainers of invisible systems: money, data and logistics flows drawn as glowing arcs. This already matches our demo. |
| **Myth-busting with calibrated honesty** ("probably not") | Most Shorts state facts flatly **[analysis]** | A signature tone: "the real answer is weirder than the myth". The phone-listening script already does this. |
| **Visible sourcing in Shorts** | Kurzgesagt publishes sourcesheets (long form). Lesics claims physics simulation. **No evidence of sourcing found for Zack D. Films or His Story** (absence of evidence, not proof). | We already produce `sources.md` per video. Publish it (pinned comment or link in bio) and show a small on-screen source chip on the key claim. Rare in Shorts, and a real differentiator. |
| **Non-uncanny 3D humans** | Zack D. leans into the "uncanny valley" (weak source) | Faceless, stylised mannequins sidestep the uncanny and the AI-slop association. |
| **Mid-cadence premium** | Volume players are at about 30 a week, premium players at about 0.2–2 a week | 3–5 a week at consistent quality is open ground. |
| **Policy risk for template channels** | YouTube's "inauthentic content" policy (renamed 2025-07-15) bars monetising "AI-generated content made with generic or unoriginal templates giving the impression of mass production" ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392)) | Our reusable world must vary visibly per episode: new props, new systems, an original script. AI disclosure note: YouTube does not require a disclosure label for "non-realistic content that's made with AI". It lists an AI-altered animation "in a fully animated video" as exempt ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491)). The page does not explicitly address a synthetic voice that is not the creator's own. Check this before launch.

## 5. Three visual directions

| | A. **Night Shift** (evolution of current look) | B. **Cutaway Diorama** | C. **The Backstage** (vertical cross-section) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Concept | The same small town, always at 2 a.m. Low-poly faceless mannequin residents sleep, scroll and shop, while the "secret" systems glow above them as light arcs, beams and particles. Every episode visits a new building on the same street, and the camera "X-rays" through walls. The night setting gives a reason why the hidden machinery is visible: everything else is dark. | A tabletop miniature world on a turntable, lit like a museum model, with tilt-shift depth of field. Each object (a fridge, card reader, toilet, ATM) is sliced in half like a cutaway exhibit, and a tiny figure walks through it. Warm, tactile, toy-like. | One tall 9:16 cross-section that uses the vertical frame. The street is at the top. Below it are floors of hidden machinery: pipes, servers, sorting belts, the "people" who route your parcel. Each episode is a new slice of the same city's underside. |
| Palette mood | Deep navy and ink (`#0b1026`) plus one hot accent per system (orange `#ff9f3a` for money, cyan `#8fd0ff` for data, red `#ff4d5e` for risk). Moonlit, calm, slightly conspiratorial. | Cream, terracotta, sage, butter yellow. Soft daylight, ambient occlusion. Friendly and trustworthy. | Concrete grey and amber work-lights above, teal "data" and copper "money" pipes below. Industrial, warm-noir. |
| Character / set | **Set:** "the Block", one street of 4–5 buildings reused forever. **Character:** "the Night Owl", a small low-poly owl (or moth) who watches from a lamppost and reacts but never talks. It's our Kurzgesagt-bird equivalent, and the brand sting can be its blink at the loop point. | **Set:** the turntable and table edge are always in frame, which gives a signature opening rotation. **Character:** "Pin", a 3 cm figure with a hard hat and a pointer, who is the scale reference and guide. | **Set:** a persistent city column with known floors. **Character:** "the Operator", a faceless worker in overalls, plus small anonymous "packet" creatures carrying data or money. |
| Differs from competitors | Zack D. is realistic and uncanny; we are stylised, faceless and calm. Branch and Jared are daylight technical renders; we are atmospheric. Our signature is invisible systems drawn as light, not mechanisms. | Bright and tactile where the Shorts 3D field skews dark **[analysis]**. Feels like a museum exhibit, which suits on-screen sourcing. | No one owns the vertical cross-section. It works natively at 9:16 and suits "systems" topics (data, money, logistics). |
| R3F build cost | **Simple to medium.** Reuse current mannequins and palette. Add: a modular street kit (instanced low-poly buildings), a wall-cutaway shader (clipping planes), an arc/particle library, bloom post-processing, and a simple owl rig (2–3 bone or vertex anims). | **Medium.** Needs good soft lighting (baked AO or `AccumulativeShadows`/`ContactShadows` from drei), a DOF/tilt-shift pass, clay-like materials and a cutaway (clipping) per object. A new model each episode is the ongoing cost. Render times are higher. | **Hard.** A large, detailed persistent set, several rigged characters with walk cycles (GLTF + `useAnimations`), a camera that cranes vertically, and many unique props per floor. Best visual payoff, but the highest ongoing cost and risk of slipping into template feel. |
| Risk | Dark palettes can blur on small, bright phone screens. Keep contrast and rim lights high. | Can read as "kids' content". | Scope creep, and it risks a "template" look if floors repeat. |

**Recommendation [analysis]:** Start with **A (Night Shift)**. It builds on what exists, and the owl witness adds a mascot at almost no cost. Borrow B's "cutaway exhibit" framing for object topics. Hold C as a later season or special format.

## Top 5 takeaways for our brand

1. **The open lane is systems, not machines.** 3D "how it works" channels own engines and gadgets. Nobody visibly owns *invisible everyday systems* (ads, pricing, data, logistics) in 3D Shorts. Our glowing-arc language already fits.
2. **Make trust the brand.** Kurzgesagt is the only big player found that publishes a sourcesheet, and none of the 3D Shorts channels showed evidence of sourcing. Ship every video with public sources and a small on-screen source chip. Keep the "probably not" honesty tone.
3. **Mascot as witness, world as anchor.** Use one persistent street at night (direction A) and a non-speaking owl that reacts, Kurzgesagt-bird style. Never let either delay the 3-second hook.
4. **Hook in 3 s, loop the ending, burn in captions.** The proposition goes in the first 3 s (TikTok guidance). Replays count as YouTube views since March 2025, so the last line should flow into the first. Keep word-synced captions, since many people watch on mute.
5. **Avoid the template trap.** YouTube demonetises "AI-generated content made with generic or unoriginal templates". The persistent world must feel visibly new every episode, with an original script and a fixed cadence (3–5 a week) rather than volume spam.
