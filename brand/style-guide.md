The look in one line: every place is **cut open and seen from outside**, with warm off-white cut faces, ink cut edges and hatch; inside it, **the objects are as real as we can build them and the people are stylized** (a recurring cast with dot eyes); the hidden system is the only orange, and it runs through the walls and the ground, out of the room and back. (One exception: a long video may open on ≈ 4 s of real footage that turns into the slice, § 11.6.1.)

**Motion principles** (owner feedback 2026-10-15, "everything feels static except the camera"; rough-cut review, "we still see both mannequins, it's kind of boring; animation is also what people want to see").
0. **The motion rule** (✅ owner decision, rough-cut review). **Something meaningful moves on screen at least every 2 to 3 s; never two people sitting still under narration. Every beat that explains a mechanism gets a visual of that mechanism** (the wake word: the phone cut open and its chip listening, § 3; a study: The Lab running it, § 3). *Meaningful* (🧪, so it can be checked): a change a viewer notices that belongs to the line: a gesture (type, lift a phone, glance, lean, point), a glide (§ 8), the route drawing or dots travelling, a mechanism animating (a chip's waveform, a robot arm tapping, the counter ticking, the meter's needle), a detail box or card entering. Breathing, blinks, the lamp and the idle drift keep a shot alive but **do not count**. Checked in Studio playback, not on stills.
1. **Something is always alive.** Figures breathe (±0.012 units, phase-offset per person), blink (eye scale y → 0.1 and back over 4 frames, every 3 to 5 s, seeded per character, the first within 3 s), nod gently, shift weight, thumbs scroll, glance (head turns up to 0.5 rad toward what the line points at), lean in to a screen, lift a phone when it lights.
2. **The set:** Night: screen light flickers on faces, the lamp breathes ±3%. Day: leaves pass the window, dust in the sun. Orange ambient life (LED pulse) only while the system is active.
3. **Calm and slow;** the orange is the only fast thing, except the "feel it" shake while the system is physically happening (§ 8 table).
4. **The motion check** (✅ owner decision, 2026-10-27; values 🧪; checked by the visual-reviewer on 3 consecutive frames per moving scene, `.claude/agents/visual-reviewer.md`). Stills hide what the owner catches on the phone; a failure is a fix, never a note.
   - **Wheels roll forward.** Spokes advance in the direction of travel, never more than half a spoke gap a frame (as built, `wheels: 'forward'`, the default: ≤ 26°/frame on a 5-spoke rim, 13° on a 10-blade one, the spokes smeared above it while the road keeps the real speed). `wheels: 'legacy'` only to re-render a posted video unchanged.
   - **Props move with the world.** Signs, markers, poles, the ruler and every prop stream past at the car's speed while it moves; nothing stays fixed beside a moving car, nothing moves once it has stopped. A parked car's world doesn't scroll.
   - **The same object across cuts.** Same body, shape, paint, lamp state (brake, hazards; hazard phase on the video clock), charge or fill state, road and time of day on both sides of every slide, glide or cut.
   - **Stopped cars show lit brake or hazard lamps.** **Open (owner to confirm):** this came from a car stopping on a road (No. 009); No. 010's cars are **parked in a charging bay with lamps off**. Recommended scope: cars that stop in traffic or break down show brake or hazard lamps; cars parked (engine off, in a bay or a driveway) show none.

### 11.6.1 The real opening: "real to section" (long-form only)

✅ **Owner decision (2026-11-02).** A long video **may** open on **at most ≈ 4 s of real stock footage**, an aerial of the story's kind of place, which then turns into our cut-open 3D version of it, seen from the same angle. **Shorts stay 100% 3D.** It is never used later in a video, and never through the story.

Every value is 🧪 until Part 1 (`datacenter-water`) is judged in Studio. Research: SF§1–5.

- The real place, seen first, makes "this is what's inside it" land.
- Stock can't show hidden systems, which is why it is never used beyond the first shot.
- It brings Content ID, logo and identifiable-company risks, and some footage looks political (main session's advice, owner's answer: "the first 3-4 second can be very good").
