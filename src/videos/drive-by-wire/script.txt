In most new cars, pressing the gas won't always speed you up.
A computer has to say yes first, so what happens when it says no?
Normally it says yes, because the pedal is only a sensor that tells the computer how far you pressed.
In a gas car, the computer tells a small motor to open the throttle.
An electric car has no throttle valve, and its computer sets how much power the motor gets.
Either way, in most cars the computer can say no, and your power gets cut while your foot's still down.
If you skid on a wet curve, stability control can cut your power by itself.
Your power can also drop when you brake hard and your shoe catches the gas pedal too.
That's brake override, which makes the car ignore or cut the gas, and it's not something to test on the road.
Steering could be next.
At least one new pickup in the US already steers by wire, with no mechanical link to the wheels.
