## Description (YouTube)

Is the gas pedal connected to the engine? In most new cars it isn't. The pedal is a sensor that tells a computer how far you pressed, and the computer decides how much power to give.

So the computer can say no. Stability control, on every new US car since the 2011 model year, can pull back engine power on its own. Brake override, on every new US car since the 2018 models, makes the car ignore or cut the gas if you press both pedals. No federal rule required it. And if part of the system fails, many cars drop into a reduced-power "limp home" mode.

Steering is starting to go that way too. At least one new pickup in the US already has no mechanical link from the wheel to the front wheels.

Illustrative: the throttle and the steering shaft are drawn larger than life, and the foot and pedal motion, the computer's yes/no pulses, the steering ratio and the speeds are exaggerated or invented. The steer-by-wire version on the main car is a what-if, the friend's electric car and the single computer box in each car are generic layouts, and the wet curve and the skid are an example, not a recorded event.
