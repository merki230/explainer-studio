## Description (YouTube)

Where does a data center's water go?

Follow the heat from one server rack on a hot afternoon, all the way up to the roof.

If your town had to choose, should its data center save water or save power?

Illustrative: the data center and its hot afternoon are a composite, not a real site. The rack, coil, pipe and rooftop-tower layout is a typical design, not the only one. The blowdown valve and chemical pumps, the space heater (assumed 1,500 watts), the fence, the crowd, the town, the Quincy plant and ponds (a generic drawing of the system the EPA describes) and the power plant are drawn to show the idea, not real equipment or places. The kettle and car-radiator comparisons are spoken analogies. "About 400 families" is our calculation: 44.3 million gallons a year divided by about 300 gallons per family per day. The opening aerial is stock footage of an unnamed data center, not the site in this story.

Towns across the US are pushing back on new data centers, and water is one of the complaints people name most. This video follows one data center's cooling water, from the server rack to the cooling tower on the roof, to show where it goes and why. Along the way it asks the questions a town council might: why not reuse the water, why not release it into a river, and why not cool without water at all.
