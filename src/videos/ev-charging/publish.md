## Description (YouTube)

Why does EV charging take so long?

Two cars, one stop, and a very different wait.

5-minute charging: game changer or overhyped?

In independent tests, a Tesla Model Y takes about half an hour to go from 10 to 80 percent. BYD says its newest cars go from 10 to 70 percent in five minutes.

Power is volts times amps. A Model Y runs on about 400 volts, so more power means more amps, and more amps means more heat in the cable and the battery. The Model Y also slows its charge as the battery fills. BYD says it uses about 1,000 volts, which gets the same power with less than half the current, and reports say its chargers push up to 1,500 amps.

In the US, BYD doesn't sell its passenger cars, and a federal rule bans connected-car software from Chinese-linked automakers starting with 2027 models. Our fastest chargers top out around 400 kilowatts for most cars, and a Model Y can use only 250. A whole stop at a gas pump, from pulling in to driving off, runs about 3 to 6 minutes.

BYD's five minutes and 1,500 amps come from the company and a press report. The only outside look at an earlier BYD system was a demo run by BYD staff, and no independent test of the newest one was found.

Illustrative: the two cars don't charge side by side in reality. The station is staged so both can be seen at once. The drivers and what they do are made up. The two BYDs cycling through one bay, the second in a different color, stand in for one car's session and the next. The time-lapse clock, the sun and clouds, and the rewind replay at 0:16 are story time. Every clock is compressed. One pulse of current on screen stands for about 250 amps. The heat glow is drawn, not measured. The "what if 2,500 amps at 400 volts" comparison is arithmetic for the same power, not a real charger. The cars, chargers and cables are generic designs, not any maker's. The dashed ghost car in the US part and the room scene are generic too.
