[calm] A gas car's engine and transmission have hundreds of moving parts.
An EV motor has about one.
[curious] Why does only the gas car need engine oil changes?
[calm] A big part of the answer is combustion.
The fuel burning inside the engine slowly wears out its oil.
Inside the engine alone dozens of metal parts slide and spin against each other.
Oil keeps that metal from rubbing and carries the heat away.
Each time the fuel burns a little hot gas leaks past the piston and down into the oil.
That gas is dirty.
It carries soot water acid and bits of unburned fuel.
Over time the acid uses up the oil's protective additives. And the fuel makes the oil thinner.
That's why many gas cars need fresh oil about every five to ten thousand miles.
An EV motor has no combustion.
Its rotor spins inside a ring of coils. And there's no engine oil to change.
Even so an EV does have oil.
A Tesla Model three's rear gearbox holds about two liters.
That oil coats spinning gears but nothing burns anywhere near them.
Tesla's maintenance schedule doesn't list a change for that oil.
In fairness that same schedule still lists brake fluid checks cabin filters and tire rotations.
Counting those one consumer study found EVs cost about half as much as gas cars to maintain and repair.
