Angle (owner-approved): nobody comes to read your water meter anymore, yet the bill knows exactly how much you used. How? A small battery-powered radio sits on the meter, often with its antenna poking through the meter-box lid by the sidewalk. It repeats the reading every few seconds, so a vehicle driving past at normal speed picks it up (AMR drive-by). Newer systems (AMI) send the reading to collectors on poles or water tanks, so nobody drives by at all.

**Answer to the brief's "verify which is common" (bubble-up vs wake-up):** bubble-up (the radio repeats on its own) is the current norm. Itron's 2014 white paper says wake-up was "50W-Series ERT modules only" (launched 2001) and that its newer modules "operate only in bubble-up mode". Neptune's current R900 sends a mobile message "every 14 seconds". Wake-up still exists in older installs (Broomfield, Middlesex Centre), so the script should describe bubble-up and not mention wake-up. See B4.

## A. How common it is

### A1. About three in four water meters in the US and Canada could be read by radio at the end of 2022; about a third were on fixed networks (AMI)

- **Script (likely):** "In the US and Canada, about three out of four water meters can now be read by radio." (Optional; the hook doesn't need a number.)
- **Status:** ✅ verified (researcher) **as attributed to a market-research firm**, North America, end of 2022. Say "about three in four" (Berg: "around 75 percent"). 🟡 for anything more recent (see note).
- **Source:** [Smart Water Metering in Europe and North America, 3rd Edition (product sheet)](https://media.berginsight.com/2023/11/25200827/bi-smartwater3-ps.pdf), Berg Insight AB, author Mattias Carlsson, published 2023-11 (accessed 2026-10-18)
- **Quote:** "North America today constitutes the leading market for both AMR and AMI solutions globally and had at the end of 2022 an installed base of 86.5 million active water utility AMR and AMI endpoints, representing a penetration of around 75 percent." / "AMI accounted for 38.0 million of the installed endpoints, equalling an AMI penetration of more than 33 percent." / "Automated meter reading (AMR) solutions which enable wireless walk- or driveby meter reading operations have over the years automated the work of meter reading personnel to some extent and are today commonplace within water metering operations."
- **Source (newer figure, not opened):** Berg Insight 4th edition (2025) press release, as republished by Business Wire / Research and Markets (403). Search snippets: end of 2024, "89.8 million" AMR+AMI endpoints, "around 80 percent" penetration, AMI "42 million", "nearly 40 percent". **Lead only.** Smart Water Magazine's copy of that release (opened, 2025-06-06) does not contain the North American numbers, only the definitions: "AMR includes metering points that require readings through drive-by or walk-by operations" and "AMI involves a fixed network communications infrastructure".
- **Note:** "Penetration" is Berg's share of all water meters. Derived (our arithmetic, not a quote): AMR alone ≈ 86.5 − 38.0 = 48.5 million, about 42% of meters; total meters ≈ 115 million. Don't speak derived figures. A 2023 regional survey (BAWSCA, Bay Area) found AMI "the predominant (44%) meter-reading method" among its member agencies (search snippet, PDF not opened); too local to use. EIA electric smart-meter numbers are **not** water; don't use them.

## B. The device on the meter

- **Source:** [Neptune R900 System Endpoints (product sheet 23-010027 PS R900 RF MIU V4 5.23)](https://dam.neptunetg.com/m/328b82e73c508b94/original/PS-ENDPOINT-R900-23-010027-05-23.pdf), Neptune Technology Group, 2023-05 (accessed 2026-10-18)
- **Quote:** "Wall endpoint: standard internal antenna" / "Pit endpoint: standard through-the-lid antenna" / "Neptune R900 wall and pit endpoints provide continuous transmission of meter data and the flexibility of mobile or fixed network reading methods."
- **Source:** [FAQ: Neptune R900 System Endpoint (FAQ R900 ENDPOINT 06.23)](https://dam.neptunetg.com/m/4318ac90cc990ab4/original/FAQ-R900-ENDPOINT-06-23.pdf), Neptune Technology Group, 2023-06 (accessed 2026-10-18)
- **Quote:** "The pit endpoint has been designed to give consistent range as long as the antenna is above the lid. Mounting below the lid will dramatically decrease range." / "If I cannot drill a 1¾” hole in the pit lid of a commercial vault, what can I do to install the antenna above the pit lid?"
- **Source:** [Electronic Reading](https://broomfield.org/1307/Electronic-Reading), City and County of Broomfield, Colorado, undated (accessed 2026-10-18)
- **Quote (tool):** "Pit ERTs are used for meters located in pits outside the building." / "The pit ERT mounts directly on or under the lid of an outdoor meter pit." / "In sidewalks, we'll use a special lid that holds the ERT underneath and out of the way of pedestrians."
- **Source:** [Advanced (Water) Metering Infrastructure FAQ's](https://newportbeachca.gov/government/departments/utilities/advanced-metering-infrastructure-project/advanced-water-metering-infrastructure-faq-s), City of Newport Beach, California, undated (accessed 2026-10-18)

That is the **focus object** for realism: a flat black-and-grey disc flush on top of the lid, the endpoint box hanging below on a coax cable. Range detail: antenna above the lid matters (Neptune FAQ), a good visual reason for the "puck".

### B3. What it is: a sealed box with a battery, a radio and a wire to the meter's electronic register

- **Script (likely):** "Inside is a battery and a small radio, wired to the meter's dial."
- **Status:** ✅ verified (researcher).
- **Source:** [Water Meter FAQs](https://middlesexcentre.ca/documents/water-meter-faq), Municipality of Middlesex Centre, Ontario, PDF created 2020-01-02 (accessed 2026-10-18)
- **Quote:** "The key to the system is a device called an ERT – Encoder-Receiver-Transmitter. This device is connected directly to a special electronic register on the water meter." / "The radio is a battery powered device."
- **Source:** [Battery Life in Water Communication Modules (white paper)](https://na.itron.com/o/commerce-media/accounts/-1/attachments/3815501), Satish Bhakta, Itron Inc., © 2014 (accessed 2026-10-18)
- **Quote:** "Itron modules use two A cell Li-SOCl2 batteries in the 50W, 60W and 100W/100W+ ERT modules." / "the 50W-series ERT module encodes accurate consumption and tamper information from the water meter"
- **Source:** Neptune FAQ, as in B1
- **Quote:** "the R900® endpoint auto-detects the type of encoder register it is connected to" / "feed the three (3) wire cable through the vault to the register." / "With the R900 endpoint, both the battery and the circuit board are fully potted in a single enclosure; thus, it is not possible to replace the battery."
- **Source:** Neptune product sheet, as in B1
- **Quote:** "Endpoint power: Lithium battery with capacitor" / "Encoder register reading interval: Every 15 minutes" / "Data logging interval: 96 days of hourly data"
- **Note:** "Potted" = sealed in resin. For the X-ray: two AA-size lithium cells (Itron: two A cells; "A cell" is larger than AA, so draw two slim cylinders, not specifically AA), a small circuit board, a coax lead up to the lid antenna, and a three-wire cable to the meter's register. Names: ERT (Itron), MIU / endpoint (Neptune). In the voice: "a small radio". The encoder register is the meter's top: it turns the mechanical count into a number the radio can read (Middlesex: "checks the reading on the meter register, encodes it into a digital signal"). Some endpoints use a "pulser" instead (Itron 60WP); skip.

### B4. It repeats the reading on its own every few seconds (bubble-up), about every 14 seconds on one common model

- **Script (likely):** "Every few seconds, all day and all night, it broadcasts the number on your meter."
- **Status:** ✅ verified (researcher) for "every few seconds" (Itron, about mobile AMR) and "every 14 seconds" **for Neptune's R900** (speak "every few seconds", or "on one common model, every 14 seconds"). ✅ for "bubble-up is the norm on newer modules" (Itron).
- **Source:** Itron white paper, as in B3, p. 4–5
- **Quote:** "AMR/AMI endpoints operate in two basic modes: wake-up and bubble-up." / "In bubble-up mode, the meter reading is transmitted continuously within the module’s frequency band." / "To support mobile AMR systems—where the vehicle drives at normal speeds—the meter-reading is transmitted every few seconds." / "Bubble-up only modules include the 60W, 200W, 100W and 100W+ ERT modules."
- **Quote (p. 6):** "To calculate battery life for the 50W series used in bubble-up mode, where the ERT transmits once every three seconds"
- **Source:** Neptune product sheet, as in B1
- **Quote:** "Transmit period (interleaved mobile and fixed network messages):" / "Standard mobile message every 14 seconds at 100 mW" / "Standard fixed network message every 7½ minutes at 1 Watt"
- **Source (third-party copy of a maker sheet, lead):** Itron 100W spec sheet hosted on a reseller domain (`alternativeenergy.yodify.com`), search snippet: "the SCM will bubble-up in this mode every 9 sec." Not opened; don't cite.
- **Note:** So the interval is 3 s (old Itron), about 9 s (Itron 100W, lead), 14 s (Neptune). "Every few seconds" is ✅ across makers. Wake-up mode (the radio sleeps until the truck calls it) is older: Itron says "Wake-Up Mode (50W-Series ERT modules only)" and that "Sending a wake-up signal requires a license with the Federal Communications Commission (FCC)." Some utilities still describe wake-up (Broomfield, Middlesex Centre, Lehigh's "pings"). Script should not say "always".

### B5. Each broadcast is short and carries the radio's own ID, which the reader matches to your address

- **Script (likely):** "Each burst lasts less than a second and carries the radio's ID number, so the truck knows which house it came from."
- **Status:** ✅ verified (researcher).
- **Source:** Middlesex Centre FAQ, as in B3
- **Quote:** "the transmissions last less than one second." / "Each radio frequency device has a unique identification number, which is transmitted along with the meter reading. The unique identification number is compared to your account record to ensure that there is a match." / "The handheld device or truck-mounted computer system matches the ERT identification number with each property and records the reading."
- **Source:** [FAQs – Extended Answers (AMI)](https://www.norfolk.gov/DocumentCenter/View/106887/AMI-FAQs---Extended-Answers), City of Norfolk, Virginia, PDF created 2025-11-07 (accessed 2026-10-18)
- **Quote:** "Each radio frequency device has a unique identification number, which is transmitted along with the meter reading." / "Personal, identifiable information, such as name, address, bill account number, or credit/collection information, is not transmitted to or from the meter."
- **Note:** The ID-not-address point is the fair side for any privacy beat: the radio sends a number and a reading; the match to your name happens in the utility's computer.
