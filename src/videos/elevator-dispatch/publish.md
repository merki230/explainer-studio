## Description (YouTube)

A computer picks which elevator comes for you, and it's not always the closest one.

Illustrative: the car positions and riders, the score values, the keypad's floor "9" and car "C", and the hotel's stop floors are examples, not real data.

Many systems pick by time instead of distance, and some also add up how much your stop would delay everyone else. Since around 1990, some buildings have had you type your floor in the lobby instead.

Sources: Richard Peters' papers on elevator dispatching (2002, 2006, 2014), NPR (2007), patents from Otis, KONE and Inventio, Schindler's 2024 annual report.
