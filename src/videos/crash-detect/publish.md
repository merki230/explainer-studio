## Description (YouTube)

How does a phone tell a crash from a drop?

Both give it a hard jolt, and only one should ever end in a call to 911.

Has your phone ever sounded that alarm when you weren't in a crash?

Apple says Crash Detection is on by default for iPhone 14 and later, and that it trained the feature on more than a million hours of real-world driving and crash record data. After a detected crash the iPhone sounds an alarm and shows an alert for 10 seconds, then counts down 30 seconds, and it calls emergency services only if you don't respond. Reporting from the first months after the iPhone 14 launched found roller coasters and ski slopes setting off automatic 911 calls, so the feature is not perfect, and Apple says it cannot detect all car crashes.

The phone and its alert, countdown and calling screens are generic drawings, not Apple's real screens. The floating phone and its cut-away are a drawing made to show the idea, not a teardown of a real iPhone, and the orange lines inside it are drawings of the signals, not real sensor readings. The held instant, where the world freezes while the sensors are explained, is a storytelling device, and the airbag is shown later than in real time. The ink waves and orange marks are drawings of sound, motion and data. The Lab and its counter are drawn, not a real place or real numbers. The coaster and ski scene is a drawn picture of the reports, not footage of any park or slope.
