Some electric cars stop at red lights without the driver touching the brake.
So what's doing the braking?
Say you're driving one, and you lift your foot as a red light comes up.
The wheels keep spinning, and now they're the ones turning the motor.
That turns the motor into a generator, and making that electricity is what slows you down.
The electricity flows back into your battery, and the car keeps slowing until you're stopped at the line.
Some electric cars can't stop all the way, and those only slow until you press the brake.
Either way, the driver behind you can't see your foot, so they're watching your brake lights.
Testers found most electric cars they checked turn those lights on by themselves when they slow firmly.
And with the motor doing so much of the slowing, your regular brakes get a lot less work.
One modeling study estimates brake wear drops by 64 to 95 percent.
There's a catch, though, since a brake supplier says discs used this rarely can rust.
Still, next time you lift your foot in an electric car and feel it pull back, that's the motor charging your battery.
