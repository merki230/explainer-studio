Agreed angle (owner): when you lift your foot in an electric car, the motor stops driving the wheels and is driven by them. It works as a generator, and that braking slows the car and sends energy back to the battery. Payoff: why the brake pads barely wear.

Owner's hook (pending verification, now checked): "Your brake lights can come on even though you never touched the brake." Question: "So how does one pedal stop the car?"

## The brake-light answer (the hook depends on it)

**Verdict: the hook holds as worded with "can".** Do not change "can" to "always" or "must".

| Where | Rule | Status |
| --- | --- | --- |
| UN Regulation 13-H (used for type approval in Europe and other UN-1958 countries; the EU publishes it in its Official Journal) | Regen on lift-off **must not** light the brake lights at or below 0.7 m/s² of deceleration, **may** light them between 0.7 and 1.3 m/s², and **must** light them above 1.3 m/s². They must go off by the time the car is slowing at less than 0.7 m/s². | ✅ (MLIT text, read directly; see the currency warning) |
| UN R13-H in 2008 (Rev. 1) | Regen on lift-off **"shall not generate"** the brake-light signal at all. The rule was later reversed. | ✅ (read directly) |
| United States (FMVSS 108) | Brake lights are required when the service brakes are applied. With regen only, they are **not required**, and NHTSA has said the standard doesn't set a deceleration level. NHTSA's 2007 letter on a different lift-off retarder says FMVSS 108 "does not prohibit" brake lights then (letter not opened; lead). | ✅ for "not required" (Consumer Reports quoting the standard and NHTSA); 🟡 for "allowed" |
| What cars do | Consumer Reports (2023, 24 EVs in its fleet): "most" lit their brake lights during hard regen. Five models from one corporate group didn't. The maker announced a free fix for about 56,000 cars. | 🟡 (tool-quoted) |

**Plain-English thresholds (our arithmetic, 🟡 derived):**

- 1.3 m/s² ≈ 0.13 g ≈ losing about **3 mph every second**.
- 0.7 m/s² ≈ 0.07 g ≈ losing about 1.6 mph every second.

Consumer Reports calls about 0.1 g "a slight but noticeable amount of braking force".

**Safe spoken wordings, from safest to strongest:**

1. ✅ "Your brake lights can come on even though you never touched the brake." (owner's hook, fine as is)
2. ✅ "Lift your foot in most electric cars and the brake lights come on, even though you never touched the brake." Needs the attribution kept in the post ("most of 24 EVs Consumer Reports tested"). Spoken "most" is 🟡 unless attributed.
3. ✅ with scope: "In Europe, once an electric car is slowing faster than about three miles an hour every second, the rules say its brake lights have to come on, brake pedal or not." Scope: UN R13-H. The EU application is via Consumer Reports' wording plus the EUR-Lex publication of R13-H.
4. ❌ Do not say "by law your brake lights come on" without a place. In the US it isn't required.

**If the owner wants an alternative hook** (the original holds, so these are optional):

- "Lift your foot off the pedal in an electric car, and the motor that was pushing you starts making electricity instead."
- "Some electric cars stop at a red light without the driver ever touching the brake."

## H. The new hook (option C): "Some electric car drivers barely touch the brake pedal all day."

Researcher, 2026-10-21. Audience 98% US, so US pages were preferred. Readability: the Nissan US newsroom release (H2), the Hyundai US owner's manual page (H1) and the Europe PMC record (H3) were read through the web tool (quotes returned as verbatim; re-quote before ✅). Tesla's US manual pages (403/404) and Chevrolet's US support pages (403) could not be opened. They are leads only, and nothing below rests on them.

**Verdict.** The hook holds as a **"can"** claim. It does not hold as a flat statement of what drivers do all day.

- **Capability (✅):** US-market manuals and a US press release say that in one-pedal modes the driver can slow down **and come to a full stop** with the accelerator alone, without pressing the brake (H1, H2, C1 2023).
- **How much of the day (✅ attributed only):** the only "share of driving" figure is a carmaker's own claim, 90% of driving needs (H2). No independent US study, survey or consumer test measures how often one-pedal drivers press the brake. I searched SAE, university, national-lab and consumer-tester sources and found none. A Hyundai SAE paper (2022-01-0645) counts paddle use, not brake presses; it's a lead only.
- **Actual behavior (🟡):** one independent field study (Germany, 80 drivers, one year, an early EV with strong lift-off regen) found conventional braking "decreased significantly" because most slowing happened through regen (H3). That supports "brake much less", not "barely all day", and it is not US data.
- **Who it holds for:** drivers of cars whose one-pedal mode stops the car fully, with the mode switched on, in normal driving (dry roads, not a full or very cold battery, no hard stops). It does **not** hold for cars that only slow on lift-off (C1, Nissan 2025), on slippery roads, with a full or cold battery (D2), or in emergency or aggressive stops (H1, H2).

## Myths and what the evidence says

| Myth | What the evidence says | Entry |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "Brake lights only come on when you press the brake pedal." | In many EVs they also come on when lifting off slows the car enough. Under UN R13-H they must above 1.3 m/s². | A1, A3, C1 |
| "There's a law everywhere that EV brake lights come on in regen." | UN R13-H requires it above 1.3 m/s². The US federal standard doesn't require it, and some EVs didn't light up until a software fix. | A1, A2, A3 |
| "Regen has always lit the brake lights." | The 2008 UN text forbade it. | A4 |
| "One-pedal cars don't have real brakes." | They have ordinary friction brakes. The rules require them to work on their own, and they blend in. | D1 |
| "Lifting off always stops the car." | Depends on the car and even the model year. One maker's 2023 manual stops the car; its 2025 manual says to brake to stop. | C1 |
| "Regen is free energy." | It only recovers part of the energy already spent getting moving. The DOE/EPA chart shows most of the gain in city driving and little on the highway. The downhill dump truck gets its energy from gravity: the load was carried up by others. | E1, G1 |
| "Regen works the same all the time." | It's reduced when the battery is full or very cold or hot. | D2 |
| "EV brakes never need attention." | Pads wear much less, but discs used rarely can rust. | F1, F3 |

## Hook facts (surprising, concrete)

1. **The car, not your foot, decides when your brake lights come on.** Under UN rules, that happens once lift-off slows the car faster than about 3 mph every second (✅ A1, 🟡 for "in Europe").
2. **The rule used to be the opposite.** In 2008 the UN text said regen "shall not" light the brake lights (✅ A4).
3. **Your motor becomes a generator when you lift off.** The wheels turn it, and making electricity is what slows you (✅ B1).
4. **The brakes are used so little they can rust,** which is why some EVs went back to drum brakes at the rear (🟡 F3).
