In China, a BYD the size of a Tesla Model 3 starts at about half the price.
Analysts who took one apart found BYD makes about three quarters of that car's parts, starting with the battery.
People tend to credit subsidies and cheap labor.
Subsidies do help, but Tesla builds the Model 3 in China too, and the teardown pointed mainly to the parts.
The battery is the most expensive part, and industry analysts put it at 30 to 40 percent of an electric car's cost.
Tesla's annual report says it buys its battery cells from suppliers like Panasonic and CATL.
BYD makes its own cells, which are long, flat iron-phosphate blades, and it builds the pack into the car's floor.
A different teardown found BYD makes its own motors and the electronics that run them, even the body panels and lights.
But that first teardown found the BYD costs only about 15 percent less to build than a Model 3 made in China.
Part of the remaining price gap comes from the cheapest trim's shorter range and China's price war.
You can't buy one in the US.
There's a 100 percent tariff, and a ban on Chinese-linked car software starts with 2027 models.
