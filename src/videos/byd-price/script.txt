In China, a BYD the size of a Tesla Model 3 starts at about half the price.
People usually assume it's subsidies and cheap labor.
Subsidies do help, but Tesla builds the Model 3 in China too.
So analysts took a BYD sedan apart, and found BYD makes about three quarters of that car's parts, starting with the battery.
The battery is the most expensive part of an electric car, and industry estimates put it at 30 to 40 percent of the car's cost.
Tesla's annual report says it buys the cells inside its batteries from suppliers like Panasonic and CATL.
BYD makes its own cells, long flat iron-phosphate blades, and builds the battery pack into the car's floor.
Engineers who took apart a smaller BYD found BYD's own motors in it too, and the electronics that run them.
Even the body panels and lights were BYD's.
Even so, the analysts estimated the sedan they took apart cost only about 15 percent less to build than a China-made Model 3.
Part of the remaining price gap is range, because the half-price BYD doesn't go as far on a charge as the Model 3.
Another part is China's price war, since BYD has been cutting prices sharply there.
You won't see those prices in the US, where BYD doesn't sell its passenger cars.
There's a 100 percent tariff on Chinese-made EVs, and starting with 2027 models, a ban on car software linked to China.
