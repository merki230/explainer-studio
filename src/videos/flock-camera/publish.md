## Description (YouTube)

How does a plate camera know your car?

Reading the plate is the old part. The newer trick is something else.

Before this video, what did you think a plate camera reads?

Illustrative: the street corner, the car, the plate 8KTR 214, the street and camera names, the search screen and the saved-record screens, the grid of training car photos and the time of day are an example, not a real camera, car or search. The pole and the camera are a generic design, not a real device. The orange boxes, the labels on the photo and the retention bar are drawings of how the data works, not what any one system's screen looks like.

A plate reader takes a photo of a passing car and turns the plate into text. The make and color are a second step: software trained on large sets of labeled car photos can learn to label them, and each sighting is saved with its labels, time and place. A search by description then asks for the saved sightings whose labels match. The company says a paper, covered or missing plate does not stop that search from finding the car. Flock's default for keeping saved sightings was thirty days and is seven for new police customers since August 2026, and the company says whoever runs the camera can choose another number, within the law.
