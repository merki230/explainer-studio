On a lot of self-checkouts, the bagging shelf is a scale.
So how does it decide something is unexpected?
Scanning the barcode pulls up what the item should weigh, a number many systems learned from the shoppers who bought it before you.
Look under the shelf, where sensors called load cells weigh the whole platform.
When you set an item down, the scale checks how much the weight went up.
If the change lands inside a window around the expected weight, you keep scanning.
Outside it, you get "unexpected item" and the machine pauses, and if you can't clear it, it calls an attendant.
Anything that changes the number counts, even a bump or a draft from the air conditioning.
And if you lift a bag off before you pay, the weight drops, which can trip the machine too.
To the scale, your groceries are numbers going up and down.
Next time it complains, look at what last touched the shelf.
