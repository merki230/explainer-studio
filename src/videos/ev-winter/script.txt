A gas car heats you with heat it was going to throw away. An EV has to make its heat from the same battery that drives it.
You've probably heard the cold drains the battery.
But in one lab test at 20 degrees, range fell 12 percent with the heater off, and about 40 percent with it on.
Most of that was the heater.
The cold slows the battery too, so braking recovers less energy.
And the heater draws power every minute, so short trips hurt most.
Test drivers lost about a quarter of their range on the highway, and earlier tests found about half on short trips.
That lost range returns in spring, without lasting harm to the battery, one study of 30,000 EVs says.
Until then, a heat pump helps by moving heat in from outside instead of making it, but less in deep cold.
Seat and steering-wheel heaters help too, but test drivers using only those got uncomfortably cold.
The usual advice is to preheat while still plugged in, so the heater runs on wall power instead of the battery.
