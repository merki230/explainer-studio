## Description (YouTube)

Why do EVs lose range in winter?

The cold gets the blame, but the biggest cost is hiding somewhere else in the car.

Do you warm your car before unplugging?

Illustrative: the cars are generic cut-away crossovers, not real models; the snowy corner, the cold test chamber, the highway, the garage, the spring street, the 8:10 AM and 7:40 AM times, the snow and frost, and the people (the driver, the test drivers, the technician) are an example, not a real place or real people. The heat pump, the orange heat flows, the range gauge and the thermometer are drawings of how the systems work, not measurements. The 12, 40, 25 and 50 percent figures are from the sources below; the 12 comes from the EPA/DOE site, which cites the same lab test.

In one lab test of five EVs at 20 degrees Fahrenheit, range fell about 12 percent with the heater off and about 40 percent with it on, so most of the loss came from heating the cabin. The cold battery costs some range too, and a heat pump, seat and wheel heaters and preheating while plugged in can each help. Other tests and fleet data give different numbers by trip, car and temperature, so your winter can differ.
