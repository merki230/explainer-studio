## Description (YouTube)

How does an insurance app know how you drive?

It doesn't need your car. Follow one ordinary drive home and see what the app counts.

Would a cheaper rate be worth having every trip scored?

Many insurance programs measure trips with the phone's own motion sensors and GPS, and the rate that comes out can go either way: State Farm says drivers can save up to 30 percent, and Progressive says only about 2 in 10 drivers get an increase (its program is not offered in every state). Consumer Reports said in 2021 that many programs count hard braking as losing 6 to 8 miles an hour in one second, and that ordinary maneuvers would register as hard braking under that range. The FTC alleged in a January 2025 complaint that General Motors collected driving data from connected cars and sold it to consumer reporting agencies. The complaint said insurers used those agencies' reports to set rates. GM settled in a January 2026 consent order, with no admission. At least one report company still advertises driving data from carmakers to insurers.

Illustrative: the drive home, the driver, the car and the 5:40 PM time are invented. The phone app "Corlo" and its screens, scores and price marker are invented. The arrow, the pins and the tick mark on the scale are drawn to show what the phone senses, not a real reading. The second car, its data path, the report company and the insurer are a generic re-enactment of the FTC's allegations against General Motors (alleged in a January 2025 complaint, settled by a January 2026 consent order with no admission). They show no real vehicle or company. The faded plug-in box is a generic outline.

- Progressive: Snapshot FAQ (undated, live 2026) https://www.progressive.com/auto/discounts/snapshot/snapshot-faq/ [company claim: trips start with no action from the user; the app "should be able to determine who is the driver"; time of day]
- Progressive: Snapshot (undated, live 2026) https://www.progressive.com/auto/discounts/snapshot/ [company claim: "only about 2 out of 10 drivers actually get an increase"; excludes NC, HI, NY and MT; not available in CA]
- State Farm: Drive Safe & Save Mobile (2026) https://www.statefarm.com/customer-care/download-mobile-apps/drive-safe-and-save-mobile [company claim: phone distraction needs a moving vehicle, a phone moving in the vehicle and the screen on; flags speed 8 mph or more over the limit; "up to 30%", may exceed 30%, NY capped; may increase or decrease at each renewal]
- Consumer Reports (Kaveh Waddell): What You're Giving Up When You Let Your Car Insurer Track You (2021) https://www.consumerreports.org/money/car-insurance/how-car-insurance-telematics-discounts-really-work-a1549580662/ [independent; a deceleration of 6 to 8 mph over 1 second in "many of the programs"; routine maneuvers "would register as hard braking"; Consumer Reports did not report an app flagging a normal stop; the article is from 2021]
- NAIC: Telematics, Insurance Topics (updated 2026-09-01) https://content.naic.org/insurance-topics/telematics [regulator: telematics measures time of day, rapid acceleration, hard braking, hard cornering]
- NAIC: Want Your Auto Insurer to Track Your Driving? (2021, modified 2025) https://content.naic.org/article/consumer-insight-want-your-auto-insurer-track-your-driving-understanding-usage-based-insurance [regulator: usage-based insurance "can also lead to higher premiums"]
- Wahlström, Skog and Händel: Smartphone-based Vehicle Telematics, A Ten-Year Anniversary, IEEE Transactions on Intelligent Transportation Systems (2017) https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1611.03618 [independent review: phones' motion sensors and GPS (about 1 Hz) in telematics; read as an arXiv copy]
- Cambridge Mobile Telematics: US patent 10,672,249, Determining, scoring, and reporting mobile phone distraction of a driver (granted 2020) https://patents.google.com/patent/US10672249 [patent: shows how a vendor can read a phone's motion sensors, not proof that a specific app does it]
- FTC: FTC Takes Action Against General Motors for Sharing Drivers' Precise Location and Driving Behavior Data Without Consent (2025-01-16) https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/01/ftc-takes-action-against-general-motors-sharing-drivers-precise-location-driving-behavior-data [regulator; allegations; the complaint PDF itself was not opened]
- FTC: FTC Finalizes Order Settling Allegations that GM and OnStar Collected and Sold Geolocation Data Without Consumers' Informed Consent (2026-01-14) https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2026/01/ftc-finalizes-order-settling-allegations-gm-onstar-collected-sold-geolocation-data-without-consumers [regulator; consent order, vote 2-0; the page has no admission language]
- LexisNexis Risk Solutions: Telematics OnDemand (page marked 2026) https://risk.lexisnexis.com/products/telematics-ondemand [company product page; shows an advertised product, not which carmakers take part or whether data flows today]
