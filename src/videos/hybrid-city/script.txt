Most gas cars get their worst mileage in stop-and-go traffic, but many hybrids get their best mileage right there.
Look at the window sticker.
On a gas car, the highway number is bigger, but on the hybrid version of the same car, the city number is bigger.
So why does a hybrid do better in town?
It wastes less energy every time it stops.
At a red light, the engine switches off.
When the light turns green, the hybrid can pull away on the electric motor.
When you brake gently, the motor turns some of the car's motion into electricity.
When you brake hard, the regular brakes take over and turn that motion into heat, as in a gas car.
Out on the highway, there's almost no idling or braking, so the hybrid has little to save.
What's left is air resistance, and it grows with the square of your speed.
So the faster you go, the harder the engine works.
Even so, the hybrid still beats its gas version on the highway, by a smaller margin.
Back in town, government figures show a very cold day can cut a hybrid's city mileage by about a third.
