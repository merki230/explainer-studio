An EV can cost less per mile than a gas car, or more.
It comes down to which plug you use.
Some people swear an EV is cheaper to run, others say it costs more, and they can both be right.
Look at a compact SUV that comes in gas and electric versions.
At today's prices, the gas one costs about fifteen cents a mile.
The electric version costs about six cents, if you charge it at home at the average US rate.
At a public fast charger, that same mile costs about seventeen cents.
Why do fast chargers cost so much more?
Many fast chargers pay the utility an extra fee set by their busiest fifteen minutes of the month.
And the average fast charger sits idle most of the day, so fewer drivers share that fee.
Wherever you charge, an EV needs no oil changes, and its brakes last longer because the motor does much of the slowing.
EVs tend to be heavier, so their tires can wear out sooner.
And the electric version also costs about six thousand dollars more to buy.
If you charge mostly at home, the savings cover that gap in about three years.
If you only use fast chargers, it can take about seventeen years.
But EVs also tend to lose value faster, and that can cut into those savings.
