## Description (YouTube)

An EV can cost less per mile than a gas car, or more.

Same SUV, same mile, and a very different bill depending on one choice.

Would you pay about $6,000 more for the electric version to save on running costs?

Illustrative: the SUV is a generic cut-away design, not a real car, and the garage, pump island, charging station, the people, the 5:40 PM time and the time-lapses are an example, not a real place. The meter's dial and needle, the orange fee drawing, the weight arrow, the tire-wear amount and the lengths of the value bars are drawings of how costs work, not measurements. The cents-a-mile and years figures are our own arithmetic from the sources below, rounded.

The per-mile figures compare an electric SUV with its gas version at the same price class. Charging at home is cheapest, and a public fast charger can cost about as much per mile as gas or more, because many fast chargers pay a fee set by their busiest 15 minutes and sit idle much of the day. Running costs are only part of the picture: the video ends on the point that EVs tend to lose value faster.

Basis of the numbers: 2026 Chevrolet Equinox EV FWD (31 kWh/100 mi, EPA rating) vs 2026 Chevrolet Equinox FWD gas (27 mpg combined, EPA rating). Gas $4.15/gal (US regular, week of Nov 2, 2026). Home electricity 18.2 cents/kWh (US residential average, Aug 2026). Fast charging 54 cents/kWh (US public fast-charging average, Q3 2026, reported through trade press, so treat it as a survey figure; it varies by state and by membership). That gives about 15 cents a mile for gas, about 6 at home and about 17 at a fast charger. Price gap: about $6,000 more for the electric version, MSRP including destination, no incentives. Catch-up years assume 15,000 miles a year, the price gap above, the fuel difference per mile, and a maintenance saving of about 3.7 cents a mile (a 2026 compact-SUV ownership-cost study: $1,954 gas vs $1,402 electric a year, tires included). On fuel alone, charging only at fast chargers never catches up; the seventeen years come entirely from the maintenance saving. No depreciation, insurance, registration or home-charger installation costs are counted in the catch-up. That ownership-cost study also finds faster EV depreciation can outweigh the savings, which is the video's last point. Prices and rates change; these are the figures on the dates given.
