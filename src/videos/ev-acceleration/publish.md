## Description (YouTube)

Are electric cars faster than gas cars? Off the line, often yes. In one published test, an electric muscle car beat a 797-horsepower gas car to 60 mph (3.3 seconds to 3.6), but the gas car reached 100 first (7.8 seconds to 8.0).

An electric motor pushes hardest from a stop, and that usually holds until around 30 mph. Past that, the push shrinks as speed climbs, while a gas car's gears keep bringing its engine back close to where it's strongest. At high speed, power per pound matters most, and the electric car had less power and was about 1,400 pounds heavier. That's why some fast EVs have a two-speed gearbox.

It wasn't a like-for-like pair: the EV had all-wheel drive, the gas car had rear-wheel drive, and the EV still won the quarter mile by a hair.

Illustrative: the two cars were tested in separate runs, shown here side by side. The gas car's speed is shaped from 60 mph so it draws level and edges ahead at 100, and the race is slowed around the 100 mph moment and in the replay. The gas car's 8-pair gearbox and the slowed engine speed are generic, as are the testers and the cars. The two-speed drive unit is a generic design, not any maker's. Arrows, meters and the chart are shapes, not measured data. The time of day is story time.
