An electric car beat a 797-horsepower muscle car to 60 miles an hour, but the gas car hit 100 first.
So why did its head start fade?
An electric motor pushes hardest from a stop, and that usually holds until around 30 miles an hour.
A gas engine makes no push until it's spinning, so it has to rev up and shift gears.
Testers timed the electric car at 3.3 seconds to 60 and the gas car at 3.6.
All-wheel drive helped the electric one.
Past about 30, the spinning motor starts fighting its own battery, so the push shrinks as speed climbs.
The gas car's push shrinks too, but each gear change brings its engine back close to where it's strongest.
Above about 30, the motor's power stays steady even though its push shrinks.
A gas car needs up to ten gears to get close to that.
At high speed, horsepower per pound matters most, and the electric car had less horsepower and was about 1,400 pounds heavier.
Porsche gave its electric Taycan's rear motor two gears, one for pulling away and one for high speed.
Next time an electric car leaves a light, watch how the gap changes as both cars speed up.
