Researcher draft, 2026-11-12. There's no script yet, so the claims follow the owner's angle in three steps. **Step 1:** GTA VI is already on pre-order customers' consoles, all of it, and it won't start. **Step 2:** how it's locked. The files arrive scrambled (encrypted), and the store sets the moment they open, one moment per store region rather than per console clock. **Step 3:** how it unlocks. Owner's angle (2026-11-12): "GTA VI is coming. People can download (pre-load) the whole game before launch but can't play it, because it's locked. How does it lock, and how does it unlock?" **The hook isn't agreed yet.** Read section H before scripting.

## 9. The idea, patented: encrypted pre-release files, and a key released at the release time, partly to spread server load (Valve, filed 2006)

- **Script (optional):** "The idea goes back to a 2006 patent: send the scrambled game early, then hand out the key when the game comes out."
- **Status:** ✅ verified (patent)
- **Source:** [US8341753B2, Managing pre-release of a game application over a network](https://patents.google.com/patent/US8341753B2/en), Valve Corp (inventors Birum, Johnson, Newcombe), filed 2006-03-08 (priority 2005-03-10), granted 2012-12-25
- **Quote (tool):** "At least one component of the application is encrypted." / "the decryption keys are made available to the client device if the pre-defined time is satisfied." / providers may spread distribution out to "reduce loads on their servers." / "Such large applications may take a considerable amount of time to download to a client device over a network." / "The intent of an early load is to minimize time to prepare the game for execution"
- **Note:** A patent describes a method. It doesn't prove what any console does. It's Valve's, so it's the PC story; keep it general ("the idea") in the voice and name the patent in the description only. The patent also allows the time check on the client, the server, or both, so it doesn't settle the clock question (claim 13).
