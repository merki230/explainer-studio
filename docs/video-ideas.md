## Testing: hidden technology inside ordinary objects (owner, 2026-10-18)

No. 002 (the radio inside the tire) drew strong comments, views and likes. The working recipe:

1. An object everyone owns but never thinks about.
2. A hidden piece of technology inside it that nobody suspects.
3. One surprise that fits in a sentence.
4. A real-life payoff the viewer can check ("next time you fill a tire…").

This is a hypothesis, not a rule: judge it on two or three more videos (metrics in `brand/feedback.md`).

## Test: "big simple" topics (owner, 2026-10-27)

**Why.** A similar account (@selfmade.engineer) reached 54.7K followers in about 15 days with 16 videos. Its hits explain big,
famous things simply: "how a car works" got 6.8M views, a Tesla Roadster video 11.5M (riding news buzz). The videos run
67–78 s, so length isn't the difference; the topic size is. Their top comments: "the most understandable way I've ever
heard", "school should be this simple".

**The test.** Make 2–3 videos on broad "how does X work" topics in our own style: the cut-open slice, verified facts, a calm
voice, one story. Keep our look and wording rather than copying theirs. Compare views, average watch time and follows
against our hidden-tech videos (No. 002, 004, 006 baseline in `brand/feedback.md`). Try big word-by-word captions on one
of them as a second variable only if the first result is clear.

**Rule of thumb.** The viewer already wonders about it ("I use this every day and have no idea how it works"). Pick from
things we already have 3D parts for when possible. Jump on a topic when it's in the news.

| Topic | Why it's big | Our existing parts | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| How a car engine turns gas into motion | Everyone drives; few know | V8Bay, Gearbox, car slice | candidate (closest to their hit; make it ours: one story, cut-open bay) |
| How your AC cools the house | Every US summer; costly bill | house/section parts | candidate |
| How a fridge makes cold | Everyone has one | kitchen/section parts (freezer idea above) | candidate |

## Parked: self-checkout bagging scale (owner, 2026-10-24)

Chosen because topics viewers argue about draw the most comments, but the video is an explanation of how it works above
all (owner). Angle: "unexpected item in bagging area": the barcode brings up an expected weight, the bagging shelf is
a scale, the machine compares the two within a margin. The familiar triggers (own bag, item left on the scanner,
leaning on the shelf, very light items) are the comparison failing. Store motives get one line at most. US setting.
Not all self-checkouts have a scale (owner has seen one at Costco), so the script says "some/many". Status: **parked** after
script (fact-check PASS) and A/V script; owner felt it lacked surprise. Files in `src/videos/self-checkout/`.

## No. 007: EV acceleration, strongest from a standstill (owner, 2026-10-24)

Owner question: is an EV quickest at the start, then fades compared with a gas car? Angle: full torque from zero
rpm wins from a red light; above the motor's base speed the push fades and one gear can't fix it, while a gas car
shifts to stay near peak power; payoff: why some fast EVs use a two-speed gearbox. Debate from viewers (EV vs gas).
No overlap with No. 006 (braking). Status: research.
